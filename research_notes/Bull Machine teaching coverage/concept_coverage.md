- The pre-fix task is not to invent context/sequence from scratch; it is to choose which already-recorded object and sequence definitions deserve source-versioned executable contracts. ## What is safe to conclude before a fix?

### Takeaway

The repository already knows the missing *kinds* of information: identified parent/range and level IDs, confirmed-at/available-at clocks, ordered state transitions, same-object direction/lifecycle, explicit expiry/invalidation, destination/room, and management bound to the entry structure. What remains is adjudicating provenance and freezing source-specific executable choices—not another generic theory campaign.

### Cited Findings

- The shared-mechanism map groups the 17 into rejection/reclaim, expansion/retest, crowding release and range-fade/timed-location, with exact facts a score cannot answer (`archetype_repair_scorecard_2026_09_30.md:132-147`).
- Current R1/R3 and parked R2 are research decisions, not authorization for a new strategy campaign; related hourly level restorations already have negative prior evidence but non-equivalence is explicitly preserved (`archetype_repair_scorecard_2026_09_30.md:189-217`).
- Economic evidence must stay separate. The dossier explicitly says clean causal detection is necessary but not proof of a profitable strategy (`archetype_translation_audit_2026_09_09.md:182-190`).

### Inferences

- A useful fix review should require, per archetype: source claim/version, executable object IDs and clocks, unresolved choices, active-code trace, and separately frozen economics. “Topic mentioned” and “feature exists” should never count as coverage.

### Gaps

- This was a whole-repository search plus detailed reading of the richest compiled sources and their referenced local notes, not a line-by-line review of every file or raw-source inventory. No claim of complete corpus mastery is warranted.
