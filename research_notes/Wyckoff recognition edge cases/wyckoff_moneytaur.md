## What source-grounded distinctions should the 12-case exam test?

### Takeaway

The strongest defensible rubric comes from combining the freshly readable contemporary post with the modern Wyckoff Analytics tutorial: require an established trading-range context, interpret a sweep through what happens next, distinguish decreasing-supply tests from demand-dominant strength, and preserve uncertainty when larger and smaller structures conflict. These are qualitative semantic contrasts, not executable thresholds.

### Cited Findings

- **Genuine spring versus isolated wick / continuation.** Wyckoff Analytics defines a spring as a late-range move below established trading-range support that reverses to close back inside; it says a spring is often followed by one or more tests, and a successful test typically makes a higher low on lesser volume. The contemporary post separately warns that taking the range low is not automatically a long and places a market-structure break and later test after the spring. — [Wyckoff Analytics, accumulation events and phases](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/); [Wyckoff Insider Model 1](https://x.com/Wyckoff_Insider/status/2097647324776239397)
- **No-spring accumulation.** The tutorial explicitly says springs are not required, shows an accumulation schematic without one, and explains that Phase-C supply testing can occur higher in the range; the current Model 2 discovery text is directionally consistent but is not primary-citable in this pass. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/); [discovery-only rendering](https://boldnet.twstalker.com/mauriceknj_)
- **Selling climax versus ongoing selloff.** A selling climax is described after a prolonged down-move with widening spread and selling pressure, often heavy volume, absorption and a close off the low; the automatic rally and subsequent secondary test help establish the range. The tutorial also says some downtrends end without dramatic climax, so absence of a single extreme bar cannot force either “climax” or “continuation.” — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)
- **Low-volume test versus failed test.** A bottom-confirming secondary test near the selling climax should show significantly reduced spread and volume; a successful post-spring test normally forms a higher low on lesser volume. If considerable supply appears on the test, the market may not be ready for markup. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)
- **SOS versus local bounce.** A sign of strength is an advance with increasing spread and relatively higher volume, often validating a prior spring; the subsequent LPS is a pullback to former resistance as support on diminished spread and volume. In Phase D, repeated SOS/LPS behavior shows demand dominance and price should at least reach the range top. A lone bullish candle or bounce inside an unresolved range is therefore insufficient. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)
- **Nested HTF/LTF conflict.** Wyckoff Insider says a complete LTF accumulation nested in larger HTF accumulation may look late locally yet early in the larger campaign. Wyckoff Analytics warns that redistribution inside a larger downtrend can initially resemble accumulation, so locally bullish-looking stopping action can remain ambiguous until the range's later evidence resolves it. — [Wyckoff Insider Model 1](https://x.com/Wyckoff_Insider/status/2097647324776239397); [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)

### Inferences

- A balanced 12-case matrix can use four positives, four near-misses and four ambiguities without inventing thresholds:
  1. **Positive spring:** established accumulation range; below-support excursion closes back inside; later higher-low/lower-volume test and SOS.
  2. **Positive no-spring accumulation:** established range; Phase-C supply test remains above the range low with contracted spread/volume; subsequent SOS and LPS.
  3. **Positive selling climax:** prolonged decline; climactic spread/volume and off-low close; automatic rally; quieter secondary test.
  4. **Positive SOS/LPS:** range evidence already exists; expanding advance reaches/breaks range resistance; diminished pullback holds former resistance.
  5. **Near-miss wick:** low pierced, but no established parent range or close back inside.
  6. **Near-miss failed recovery:** apparent spring closes inside, then loses the candidate low or shows renewed supply before a valid test/SOS.
  7. **Near-miss local bounce:** bullish bars occur, but spread/volume and location do not demonstrate SOS or escape the range.
  8. **Near-miss false climax:** large down bar during an intact decline, followed by continued downside without AR/ST range formation.
  9. **Ambiguous non-climactic stop:** decline loses momentum without a dramatic SC; allow “unresolved,” not forced accumulation.
  10. **Ambiguous test:** price retests support but volume/spread evidence is mixed or unavailable.
  11. **Ambiguous nested conflict:** LTF accumulation sequence sits inside an HTF redistribution/downtrend; preserve both labels and withhold a universal directional verdict.
  12. **Ambiguous local strength:** local SOS-like expansion occurs but does not reach the parent range boundary or is contradicted by continuing HTF supply.
- Each case should expose the parent range and its timeframe, candidate and confirmation closes, volume/spread comparisons, and which later evidence was available at the adjudication cutoff. A label should separate `structural event`, `phase interpretation`, and `directional permission`; the sources do not make those identical.
- For symmetry, a later expansion could mirror the same contrasts for distribution (UT/UTAD, SOW/LPSY). The tutorial expressly says UTAD is optional and defines SOW/LPSY using location, spread and volume rather than a wick alone. — [Wyckoff Analytics](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wyckoff-method/)

### Gaps

- Neither source specifies numerical volume ratios, candle-count windows, allowable penetration depth, recovery bars, parent-boundary algorithms, or how to aggregate hourly candles. Those remain project choices and must not be reverse-engineered from fixtures.
- The sources do not define a deterministic rule for resolving HTF bearish versus LTF bullish evidence. The exam should allow conflict/unknown rather than smuggling in a precedence rule.
- Images attached to the X teachings were not inspected. The proposed cases are based on accessible text and the tutorial's textual event definitions, not on inferred chart geometry.

## What do these sources not establish?

### Takeaway
